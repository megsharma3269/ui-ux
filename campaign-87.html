What if one number changed everything?

People don't know how stressed they are. They say "I'm fine" fourteen times a day. They wear their exhaustion like a badge. And they have no idea that right now, in this moment, their body is running at 87 out of 100 on the stress scale — and has been for weeks.

Don't launch a product. Launch a number.

Weeks 1 – 2 · The Number

Nobody Knows What 87 Means. Yet.

Mysterious billboards appear in London, Tokyo, New York, Mumbai, and São Paulo. Social ads flood feeds. Influencer posts. Guerrilla projections on buildings at night. They all show the same thing: the number 87 in red on black. No logo. No product. No explanation. Nothing but two digits and a growing wave of speculation.

People search "what is 87." Reddit threads spiral. News outlets run "the mystery of 87" segments. The number becomes a cultural object before anyone knows it belongs to Luna.

Weeks 4 – 6 · The Movement

What's Your Number?

Pop-up "Stress Stations" appear in high-traffic locations. Step in, put on a Luna ring for sixty seconds, and get your real stress number. The reveal moment — when someone who said "I'm fine" sees their actual score — is filmed with consent and shared as a social series. People start sharing their numbers the way they share personality types and horoscope signs: "I'm a 72." "I got mine down to 54 after the breathing exercise."

The number becomes a social object. Templates for Instagram stories. TikTok creators comparing numbers. Morning shows testing their hosts live on air. "What's your number?" enters the cultural lexicon as a wellness check-in, not a pickup line.

Why numbers go viral

People share Wordle grids, Spotify Wrapped stats, and MBTI types for the same reason: a number or label gives language to something personal. "I'm a 72" is more specific than "I'm kind of stressed," more shareable than a journal entry, and more interesting than a product feature list.

Luna doesn't need to convince people to talk about the product. The number IS the conversation. Every shared score is an organic ad that puts AI-Stress Index in front of a new audience — with social proof built in.

Activation ideas

"Check Your Number" challenge on TikTok · Morning-show live tests · Corporate wellness pop-ups ("Your office's average: 83") · City leaderboards · Couples comparing numbers · "Number drop" events at music festivals

Why 87 Wins

Mystery drives earned media

Phase 1 generates press, social speculation, and organic search volume before Luna spends a dollar on branded advertising. The reveal converts all that attention into product awareness overnight.

A number is a social object

People share scores compulsively — Wordle, Wrapped, personality tests. "What's your number?" gives Luna a viral mechanic that's native to how people already behave on social platforms.

The product demo IS the campaign

Every pop-up station, every shared score, every live TV test is a real demonstration of AI-Stress Index. The campaign doesn't describe the product — it IS the product, experienced for the first time.

Global, culture-agnostic

A number needs no translation. 87 means the same thing in Tokyo, Mumbai, London, and São Paulo. The campaign scales globally without localization, and city-specific data hooks create local relevance.
